What is PV (Photovoltaics)?
Solar

PV, or photovoltaics, is the direct conversion of sunlight into electricity using semiconductor cells. A PV system layout defines how modules, strings, inverters, and wiring connect to safely deliver that power to a load or the grid.

How to Read a Single-Line Diagram (SLD)
Solar / Electrical

An SLD simplifies a multi-conductor electrical system into a single line per connection. For solar, it typically shows the PV array, combiner boxes, inverter(s), disconnects, metering, and the point of grid interconnection — the whole power path at a glance.

CCTV System Architecture, Briefly
Electrical

A typical CCTV system layout maps camera placement for coverage, cable runs (often PoE) back to a switch, and a recorder/NVR for storage. Layout decisions balance coverage, cable length limits, and power budget.

PoE (Power over Ethernet), Briefly
Electrical / Networking

PoE delivers power and data over the same Ethernet cable, which is why it's common for CCTV cameras and access points — one cable run instead of two.

UART vs I²C vs SPI
Embedded Systems

UART is a simple two-wire, point-to-point serial link. I²C uses two shared wires and addresses to talk to multiple devices on one bus. SPI uses separate clock/data lines and chip-select signals for faster, multi-device communication. The right choice depends on speed, wiring, and how many devices need to share the bus.

ADC & GPIO, Briefly
Embedded Systems

GPIO pins are general-purpose digital in/out. An ADC (analog-to-digital converter) lets a microcontroller read analog signals — like a sensor's varying voltage — by converting them into digital values it can process.

ESP32 vs Raspberry Pi
Embedded Systems

ESP32 is a low-power microcontroller great for sensor nodes and Wi-Fi/Bluetooth IoT devices. Raspberry Pi is a full single-board computer, better suited for tasks needing an operating system, like computer vision or running local services.

Fiber Communication, Briefly
Communication

Optical fiber carries data as light pulses instead of electrical signals, allowing much higher bandwidth and longer distances with lower loss than copper — the backbone of most long-distance and high-speed networks.
