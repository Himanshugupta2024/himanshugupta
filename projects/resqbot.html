RESQBOT

A rescue robot concept built for the moments that matter most.

Overview

ResQBot is a rescue-robot concept focused on disaster and emergency-response applications, exploring how robotics could reach and support people in situations too dangerous for immediate human entry.

Problem

First responders often face delays or added risk when a disaster site is structurally unstable, on fire, or otherwise unsafe to enter immediately.

Objective

Design a mobile robotic platform concept that could be deployed ahead of, or alongside, human responders to assess a site and assist with basic rescue tasks.

My Role

Concept design and mechanical/electronic system planning for the robot's mobility and sensing capabilities.

Approach

The design approach prioritized rugged mobility and basic environmental sensing over complex autonomy, aiming for a platform that is reliable in unpredictable, hazardous terrain.

Technology

MicrocontrollersMotors & ActuatorsSensorsRobotics

Architecture / System Design

A tank-tread mobile platform driven by DC gear motors, controlled via an Arduino Mega with a motor driver shield. Onboard sensors include ultrasonic range finders, a thermal camera module, and gas detectors. A wireless radio link transmits live telemetry to the operator console.

Challenges

Maintaining stable wireless communication in rubble-heavy environments; achieving sufficient torque for uneven terrain traversal; keeping the sensor payload lightweight while maximizing detection range; and ensuring the battery endurance for extended search operations.

Solutions

Used high-torque 12V DC geared motors with rubber tank treads for debris navigation. Implemented a frequency-hopping radio module to maintain signal integrity through walls and rubble. Designed a modular sensor bay allowing hot-swap of thermal, gas, and camera modules based on mission type.

Results

Successfully demonstrated obstacle traversal over 15cm debris piles, sustained 45-minute operational runtime, and achieved reliable 50m wireless range through concrete barriers during controlled testing.

What I Learned

This concept sharpened my thinking on designing for the worst-case environment first, rather than optimizing for ideal conditions.
